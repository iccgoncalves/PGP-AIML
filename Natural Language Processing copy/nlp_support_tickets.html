Context & Objectives

In today's dynamic business landscape, organizations are increasingly recognizing the pivotal role customer feedback plays in shaping the trajectory of their products and services. The ability to swiftly and effectively respond to customer input not only fosters enhanced customer experiences but also serves as a catalyst for growth, prolonged customer engagement, and the nurturing of lifetime value relationships. As a dedicated Product Manager or Product Analyst, staying attuned to the voice of your customers is not just a best practice; it's a strategic imperative.

While your organization may be inundated with a wealth of customer-generated feedback and support tickets, your role entails much more than just processing these inputs. To make your efforts in managing customer experience and expectations truly impactful, you need a structured approach – a method that allows you to discern the most pressing issues, set priorities, and allocate resources judiciously. One of the most effective strategies at your disposal is to harness the power of Support Ticket Categorization.

Objective

Develop an advanced support ticket categorization system that accurately classifies incoming tickets, assigns relevant tags based on their content, implements mechanisms and generate the first response based on the sentiment for prioritizing tickets for prompt resolution.

# defining a function to parse the JSON output from the model
def extract_json_data(json_str):
    try:
        # Find the indices of the opening and closing curly braces
        json_start = json_str.find('{')
        json_end = json_str.rfind('}')

        if json_start != -1 and json_end != -1:
            extracted_category = json_str[json_start:json_end + 1]  # Extract the JSON object
            data_dict = json.loads(extracted_category)
            return data_dict
        else:
            print(f"Warning: JSON object not found in response: {json_str}")
            return {}
    except json.JSONDecodeError as e:
        print(f"Error parsing JSON: {e}")
        return {}

# Defining the response function for Task 1.
def response_1(prompt, ticket):
    model_output = llm(
        f"""
        Q: {prompt}
        Support ticket: {ticket}
        A:
        """,
        max_tokens=150,  # Setting the maximum number of tokens to 150
        stop=["Q:", "\n"],
        temperature=0.5,  # Setting temperature to 0.5
        echo=False,
    )

    # extracting the output from the model response
    temp_output = model_output["choices"][0]["text"]
    final_output = temp_output[temp_output.index('{'):]

    return final_output

max_tokens=150

Reasoning: The max_tokens parameter determines the maximum length of the response generated by the model. Setting it to 150 allows the model to generate a sufficiently detailed and informative answer without being overly verbose. This length is typically adequate for addressing support ticket inquiries comprehensively while ensuring the response remains concise and to the point.

Impact: This setting ensures that each ticket gets a well-rounded response without the risk of being too verbose. A higher value could lead to unnecessarily long responses, while a lower value might result in incomplete or overly brief answers.

temperature=0.5

Reasoning: The temperature parameter controls the randomness and creativity of the model's output. With a limited dataset of 21 support tickets, maintaining a balance between creativity and coherence is crucial. A temperature of 0.5 helps generate responses that are varied enough to cover different nuances in the support tickets, while still being relevant and coherent.

Impact: A higher temperature (closer to 1) would make the responses more diverse but potentially less relevant and coherent. A lower temperature (closer to 0) would make the responses more deterministic and repetitive, which might not be ideal for all queries.

# prompt engineering for ticket categorization
prompt_1 = """
    You are a technical assistant. Classify the support ticket based on the Support Ticket Text presented
    in the input into the following categories and not any other.
      - Technical issues
      - Hardware issues
      - Data recovery
      - Other (if the ticket doesn't fit into any of the above categories)
    It is important that every support ticket is classified. If you are unsure or the ticket does not clearly fit into any of the categories, classify it as "Other".
    Return only a structured JSON output in the following format:
    {"Category": "category_prediction"}
    """

The prompt structure is set in a way that looks to support clarity, precision, and relevance in the model's task. By defining the role as a "technical assistant" and specifying the task of classifying support tickets into three distinct categories — Technical issues, Hardware issues, and Data recovery — the prompt focuses the model's response, ensuring it addresses only the relevant classifications. Requiring the output in a structured JSON format enhances the efficiency and consistency of the responses, making them easily parsable and suitable for integration into automated systems or further processing.

We've set a fallback mechanism to support debugging where IF the model were to not be able to classify an instance THEN it should classify it as 'other'.

Business Insights & Recommendations

Project Objectives: The primary objective was to develop an advanced support ticket categorization system that accurately classifies tickets, assigns tags, and generates initial responses based on sentiment. This aims to streamline the customer support process and enhance customer satisfaction.

Key Findings:

  • Categorization Accuracy: The model appears to be effectively categorizing the support tickets presented in this dataset. That said, we'd like to once again caution for the importance of a more complex dataset in future prompt evaluation.
  • Response Generation: The system seems capable of drafting responses that align with the sentiment of the ticket, aiding in prompt resolution. Further evaluation however is required in ensuring that we're adequately assessing the model's output with ethics, equity, and regulatory/legal compliance in place. Ensuring that the model prioritizes the wellbeing and safety of our users is paramount.
  • Priority Setting: Tickets seem to be correctly prioritized by the mmodel, ensuring urgent issues are addressed swiftly. Once again, however, we'd like to highlight that how we define certain classification elements such as our priotization approach matters in how the model may balance at times competing user and business needs.

Potential Benefits:

  • The categorization system may help reduce the time required for support agents to sort and prioritize tickets.
  • Automated responses may ensure customers receive timely acknowledgments, which can lead to improved satisfaction.
  • By helping identify ticket priorities, resources may be more effectively allocated, with focus on high-impact issues.

Business Recommendations

  • Continuous Prompt Evaluation: Thoroughly evaluating the model against a robust responsible AI protocol is essential to ensure the safety and security of our users.

  • Internally defining labels: A close collaboration with key stakeholder is paramount in appropriately reflecting and defining some of the labels against which we wish to adjust the prompt. As mentioned, the way we define an element such as 'priority' can radically shift how the model responds and how closely aligned it is with user and business needs.

  • Integration with CRM Systems: Integrating the ticket categorization system with existing CRM platforms could be valuable in streamlining workflows and enhance data accessibility.

  • Feedback Loop: Implementing a feedback mechanism for support agents to provide insights on the model's performance could be incredibly beneficial in allowing for ongoing improvements.

  • Comprehensive Reporting: Developing detailed reports on ticket trends and response times could be valuable to identify areas for further enhancement in the support process.

  • Customer Follow-up: Ensuring automated follow-ups based on ticket resolution could be helpful in maintaining customer engagement and gather feedback on the support experience.

Conclusion

Implementing the support ticket categorization system has shown significant potential in improving operational efficiency and customer satisfaction. By continuing to refine the prompt and leveraging insights from customer interactions, the organization can enhance its support capabilities, fostering stronger customer relationships and driving long-term business success.
